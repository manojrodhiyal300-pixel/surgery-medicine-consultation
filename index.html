Critical Educational Awareness

Key Cancer Warning Signs You Should Never Ignore

Early detection significantly improves treatment outcomes. If you experience persistent or unexplained red flag symptoms, seek professional medical evaluation immediately.

Unexplained Weight Loss

Losing 5% or more of body weight over 6-12 months without dieting or lifestyle change needs medical investigation.

Unexplained Bleeding

Blood in stool, urine, cough, or unusual discharge warrants immediate diagnostic evaluation.

New Lumps or Swellings

Hard, growing, or persistent lumps in the breast, neck, armpit, groin, or soft tissues should be examined by a doctor.

Persistent Symptoms

Non-healing ulcers, persistent cough/hoarseness, or swallowing difficulty lasting over 2–3 weeks.

GI & Digestive Oncology

Gastrointestinal (GI) Cancer Overview

Includes cancers of the esophagus, stomach, liver, gallbladder, pancreas, colon, and rectum.

Warning Signs: Persistent indigestion, black/bloody stools, jaundice, unexplained weight loss.

Key Tests: Endoscopy, Colonoscopy, CT Scan, Biopsy.

Book Consultation
Gastric Care

Stomach / Gastric Cancer

Malignant growth originating in the mucosal lining of the stomach.

Warning Signs: Persistent upper abdominal pain, early satiety (feeling full quickly), dark vomiting or tarry stools.

Key Tests: Upper GI Endoscopy (UGIE) + Biopsy, CT Abdomen.

Book Consultation
Colorectal Health

Colon, Rectal & Colorectal Cancer

Cancer affecting the large intestine or rectum, often arising from pre-existing adenomatous polyps.

Warning Signs: Change in bowel habits (alternating constipation/diarrhea), blood mixed in stool, unexplained anemia.

Key Tests: Colonoscopy + Biopsy, CEA blood test, CT/MRI.

Book Consultation
Esophagus Care

Esophageal Cancer

Tumors arising in the muscular tube connecting throat to stomach.

Warning Signs: Progressive difficulty swallowing (dysphagia), food sticking in chest, painful swallowing.

Key Tests: Upper Endoscopy, Barium Swallow, PET-CT.

Book Consultation
Hepatobiliary & Pancreas

Liver, Gallbladder & Pancreatic Cancer

Complex malignancies of the liver (HCC), bile ducts (cholangiocarcinoma), gallbladder, or pancreas.

Warning Signs: Painless progressive jaundice (yellowing eyes/skin), dark urine, pale stools, upper back/abdominal pain.

Key Tests: Triple Phase CT, MRCP, LFT blood panel, CA 19-9 marker.

Book Consultation
Breast, Lung & Head/Neck

Breast, Lung, Oral & Urological Cancers

Includes breast lumps, lung nodules, oral non-healing ulcers, prostate, bladder, and kidney growths.

Warning Signs: Firm painless lumps, hemoptysis (blood in sputum), hematuria (blood in urine), mouth ulcers over 2 weeks.

Key Tests: Mammography, Core Biopsy, PSA, Cystoscopy, CT Chest.

Understanding Investigations

Diagnostic Test Information

Learn about common diagnostic scans, blood tests, and invasive investigations in patient-friendly terms.

Ultrasound (USG) Scan

Non-invasive scan using sound waves to visualize abdominal organs, gallbladder, thyroid, pelvis, and soft tissues.

Used For: Gallstones, fatty liver, hernia, kidney stones, pelvic masses.

What to Ask: "Do I need to fast before the scan?"

Computed Tomography (CT Scan)

3D cross-sectional X-ray imaging providing detailed views of organs, blood vessels, and internal lesions.

Used For: Tumor staging, appendicitis, liver/pancreas evaluation, chest scans.

What to Ask: "Is IV contrast required, and should serum creatinine be checked?"

Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)

Uses magnetic fields and radio waves to create high-resolution images of brain, spine, joints, rectal canal, and soft tissues.

Used For: Brain/spinal disorders, rectal cancer staging, fistula, joint cartilage.

What to Ask: "Are metal implants or claustrophobia a concern?"

Endoscopy & Colonoscopy

Direct visual examination of food pipe/stomach (Endoscopy) or large intestine/rectum (Colonoscopy) using a flexible camera.

Used For: Ulcers, GERD, bleeding sources, colon polyps, biopsy sampling.

What to Ask: "What preparation or bowel cleansing is needed?"

Biopsy & Histopathology

Removal of a small tissue sample for laboratory microscopic examination to confirm whether a lump or lesion is benign or cancerous.

Used For: Definitive cancer diagnosis, tissue grading, immunohistochemistry (IHC).

What to Ask: "How many days will the histopathology report take?"

PET-CT & Mammography

Advanced radioactive tracer imaging for full-body cancer staging (PET-CT) and low-dose X-ray breast screening (Mammography).

Used For: Whole-body disease extent, treatment response, early breast lesions.

What to Ask: "When is PET scan appropriate compared to standard CT?"

Proactive Wellness

Preventive Health & Screening

Preventive checks detect hidden risk factors and early-stage diseases before symptoms occur.

Metabolic Screening

Regular blood pressure checks, Fasting Blood Sugar/HbA1c tests, and Lipid Profile screening to prevent heart disease and stroke.

Cancer Screening

Mammography/USG for breast health, Pap smear for cervical screening, and Colonoscopy or Stool Occult Blood testing for colorectal health.

Lifestyle Risk Control

Tobacco cessation support, alcohol reduction guidance, balanced nutrition, and weight management strategies to lower cancer and metabolic risks.

When Screening Matters

Adults over 40, or individuals with a strong family history of cancer, heart disease, or diabetes should seek regular physician consultations.

Emergency Notice

If you have severe, sudden, or life-threatening symptoms (such as acute severe chest pain, extreme breathlessness, sudden weakness/paralysis, heavy active hemorrhage, or loss of consciousness), seek emergency medical care at the nearest hospital immediately rather than waiting for a consultation.
